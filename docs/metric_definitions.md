# Metric Definitions

This document defines the key financial and risk metrics used in the banking peer comparison analysis.

The metrics are grouped according to the analytical dimensions used in the project.

---

# Balance Sheet Metrics

## Total Assets

Total assets represent the entire asset base of the bank, including loans, securities, cash reserves and other financial assets.

This metric provides a high-level indicator of the bank's overall scale.

---

## Total Loans

Total loans represent the bank’s lending portfolio to retail, corporate, and commercial customers.

Loans are typically the primary income-generating assets of banks.

---

## Total Deposits

Total deposits represent the primary funding source for most banks.

Deposits include customer accounts such as:

- demand deposits
- savings accounts
- time deposits

---

## Equity

Equity represents the bank’s capital base contributed by shareholders plus retained earnings.

It acts as a loss absorption buffer against financial stress.

---

# Profitability Metrics

## Net Profit

Net profit represents the bank’s total earnings after deducting:

- operating expenses
- loan loss provisions
- taxes

It is the most direct indicator of financial performance.

---

## Return on Equity (ROE)

ROE measures the profitability generated from shareholder capital.

Formula:

ROE = Net Profit / Equity

Higher ROE indicates more efficient capital utilization.

---

## Return on Assets (ROA)

ROA measures how efficiently the bank generates profit from its asset base.

Formula:

ROA = Net Profit / Total Assets

Because banks are highly leveraged institutions, ROA values are typically relatively small.

---

## Net Interest Margin (NIM)

Net Interest Margin measures the profitability of the bank’s core lending activity.

Formula (simplified):

NIM = Net Interest Income / Interest Earning Assets

Net interest income equals interest earned on loans and securities minus interest paid on funding sources.

Note:  
Different banks may report NIM with slightly different methodologies, such as swap-adjusted or swap-cost-included calculations.

---

# Credit Risk Metrics

## Non-Performing Loan Ratio (NPL Ratio)

NPL ratio measures the proportion of loans that are in default or close to default.

Formula:

NPL Ratio = Non-Performing Loans / Total Loans

Higher values indicate deteriorating credit quality.

---

## Stage 3 Coverage Ratio

Stage 3 coverage measures the provisioning buffer against impaired loans.

Formula:

Stage 3 Coverage = Loan Loss Provisions / Stage 3 Loans

Higher coverage indicates stronger loss absorption capacity.

---

## Cost of Risk (CoR)

Cost of Risk measures the credit loss cost relative to the loan portfolio.

Formula:

Cost of Risk = Loan Loss Provisions / Total Loans

It reflects the flow of credit losses during a given period.

---

# Capital Adequacy Metrics

## CET1 Ratio (Common Equity Tier 1)

CET1 represents the highest quality regulatory capital.

Formula:

CET1 Ratio = CET1 Capital / Risk Weighted Assets

This ratio measures the bank’s core capital strength and its ability to absorb losses.

---

## Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)

CAR measures the total regulatory capital relative to risk weighted assets.

Formula:

CAR = Total Regulatory Capital / Risk Weighted Assets

Banks must maintain CAR levels above regulatory minimum requirements.

---

# Liquidity Metrics

## Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR)

LDR measures the proportion of loans funded by deposits.

Formula:

LDR = Total Loans / Total Deposits

Interpretation:

- Lower LDR → stronger liquidity position
- Higher LDR → tighter funding conditions

---

# Interest Rate Risk Metrics

## IRRBB (Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book)

IRRBB measures the sensitivity of a bank’s economic value to changes in interest rates.

It reflects how the bank’s balance sheet reacts to interest rate shocks.

Lower IRRBB values indicate lower exposure to interest rate fluctuations.

---

# Growth Metrics

## Loan Growth (YoY)

Loan growth measures the annual expansion of the bank’s lending portfolio.

Formula:

Loan Growth = (Loans_t − Loans_{t-1}) / Loans_{t-1}

Higher growth may signal stronger market expansion but can also indicate higher potential risk accumulation if not accompanied by adequate risk controls.

---

# Derived Analytical Ratios

Several additional ratios are derived during the analysis to understand balance sheet structure.

## Loans / Assets

Measures how much of the asset base consists of loans.

Higher values indicate a more lending-focused balance sheet.

---

## Deposits / Assets

Measures the importance of deposits as a funding source.

Higher values indicate stronger deposit funding stability.

---

## Equity / Assets

Measures the proportion of assets funded by equity capital.

Higher ratios indicate stronger capital buffers.
